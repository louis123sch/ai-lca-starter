Hermesmann and Müller consider four hydrogen production technologies: steam methane reforming (SMR), steam methane reforming with carbon capture and storage (SMR-CCS), methane pyrolysis (MP), and polymer electrolyte membrane electrolysis (PEMEL). The study considers nine process configurations in total.

[PAGE 4]
TECHNOLOGY REVIEW — NOT THE HARMONIZED LCI TABLE.
The SMR process description discusses feed pre-treatment, steam reforming, water-gas shift and final purification by pressure swing adsorption. It also discusses possible catalysts such as nickel, Ni/Al2O3, Ru/ZrO2, Fe/Cr, Co/Mo and CuO/ZnO. These review details describe the technology and must not automatically be treated as separate foreground LCI processes or quantified inventory flows.

[PAGE 6]
TECHNOLOGY REVIEW — NOT THE HARMONIZED LCI TABLE.
The methane-pyrolysis review describes methane cleavage, removal of solid carbon, and gas purification. It discusses plasma, packed-bed, fluidized-bed, monolithic, liquid-bubble-column and moving-bed reactor concepts and catalyst families including nickel, iron, cobalt, carbonaceous materials and molten media. The LCA itself later selects a liquid bubble column reactor using tin as molten medium.

3. LIFE CYCLE ASSESSMENT — GOAL AND SCOPE
The LCA compares four H2 production processes: SMR, SMR-CCS, methane pyrolysis and PEM electrolysis, with nine different configurations. The scope is cradle-to-gate: resource extraction through hydrogen production at the factory gate. Hydrogen transport and storage after production are omitted. The functional unit is 1 kg H2 at 30 bar pressure available at the production site.

Foreground configurations represented by the study:
- SMR
- SMR-CCS with 56% capture
- SMR-CCS with 90% capture
- MP-H2: methane pyrolysis with heat supplied from hydrogen
- MP-C: methane pyrolysis with heat supplied from carbon
- MP-NG: methane pyrolysis with heat supplied from natural gas
- MP-E: methane pyrolysis with heat supplied from electricity
- PEMEL today
- PEMEL future

The SMR-CCS study combines SMR production with CO2 sequestration, compression, transport and geological storage. The two modeled capture configurations are approximately 56% pre-combustion capture and 90% post-combustion capture. These are configurations of SMR-CCS, not separate hydrogen-production technologies.

[PAGE 13]
For methane pyrolysis, the LCI refers to hydrogen production in a liquid bubble column reactor using tin as molten medium. Four heat-supply configurations are modeled: hydrogen, natural gas, carbon and electricity. The by-product carbon is considered separately in the study's avoided-burden analysis; Table 3 is the harmonized foreground LCI used for the process configurations.

For PEMEL, present-day production assumes 55 kWh/kg H2 and future production 45 kWh/kg H2. The LCI values for the two PEMEL configurations are in Table 4.

3.2.6 BACKGROUND SYSTEM
The study uses ecoinvent v3.6 cut-off for the background system. For domestic H2 production in Germany, regional dataset priority is Germany (DE), Europe (RER), Europe without Switzerland (EWS), global (GLO), then rest of world (RoW). For country-specific results, natural-gas and electricity datasets are adapted to the respective country grid mixes.

REFERENCE CASE
The reference case represents present-day conditions in Germany. Electricity is supplied from the German grid mix and natural gas from the corresponding grid/market supply chain.
