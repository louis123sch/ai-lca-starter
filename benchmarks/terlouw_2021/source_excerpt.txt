[DOCUMENT: Terlouw et al. 2021, Environmental Science & Technology 55, 11397-11411]

The study applies attributional LCA using ecoinvent 3.6, allocation cut-off by classification, and Brightway2. The functional unit is the gross removal of 1 ton CO2 from the atmosphere via a DAC plant combined with geological CO2 storage. The main analysis uses a near-future DAC unit with 100 kt CO2/year capture capacity and a 20-year system lifetime.

Five alternative DACCS energy-system layouts are evaluated:
1. Autonomous (Fresnel + PV): PV electricity, Fresnel solar heat, battery electricity storage and heat storage.
2. Autonomous (HTHP + PV): PV electricity, high-temperature heat pump (HTHP), battery storage.
3. HTHP + Grid: HTHP supplied by grid electricity.
4. Waste Heat + Grid: waste heat plus grid electricity.
5. Waste Heat + PV + Battery: waste heat plus PV electricity and battery storage.

Battery pack capacity: 125 MWh for Autonomous Fresnel+PV, 221 MWh for Autonomous HTHP+PV, 0 for HTHP+Grid, 0 for Waste Heat+Grid, and 120 MWh for Waste Heat+PV+Battery. HTHP size is 17 MW in Autonomous HTHP+PV and HTHP+Grid. Heat storage is about 210 MWh heat for Autonomous Fresnel+PV. Fresnel size is about 77-108 MWp heat depending on location. PV capacities are location-specific ranges.

The DACCS foreground explicitly includes the DAC plant and operation and, depending on configuration, energy generators and storage units. CO2 storage/transport includes compression, pipeline transportation, recompression where applicable, injection, infrastructure requirements and CO2 leakage during transport. The paper states that these are foreground processes. For the configuration-level benchmark below, these explicit shared foreground activities are retained as named foreground exchanges within each assessed layout rather than being converted to background ecoinvent dataset names.

The study considers multiple countries; autonomous solar layouts are evaluated in Chile, Greece, Jordan, Mexico and Spain, while grid-coupled alternatives also include Switzerland, Iceland and Norway. No single country should replace the multi-country study context.
